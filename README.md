[Exploratory tests on Crimson Roman for legal and academic workflows]

This repository contains a series of exploratory tests performed on Crimson Roman in the context of legal and academic writing. The samples were produced in FontForge and evaluated both on‑screen and in print, with the goal of understanding how Crimson behaves under digital‑first workflows and low‑quality office printing.

These observations are not typographic doctrine, but practical, user‑level findings supported by AI‑assisted analysis (Microsoft Copilot). The tests focus on paragraph colour, x‑height behaviour, Roman/Italic balance, and the robustness of OTF and TTF rendering.

The adjustments shown here are exploratory drafts, intended to illustrate the challenges encountered when using Crimson in long‑form legal text and the configurations that produced the most promising results. Among the tested variants, the configuration using Style 2, x‑height 450, 102% uniform scaling, and TrueType export demonstrated the most balanced performance in terms of legibility, paragraph colour, and print robustness.

These results should be interpreted as practical observations, not final design proposals. They suggest that Crimson has significant potential for professional legal and academic contexts if revisited with contemporary production realities in mind. Any refinement or redesign would naturally benefit from expert typographic guidance and a deeper understanding of Crimson’s design philosophy.

The full exploratory document is available in the PDF included in this repository.
